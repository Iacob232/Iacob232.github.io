### “I know me”
The other day, when I was with Marie,<br>
while we were walking in the nearby park,<br>
we came across a goose without a leg.<br>
Marie then, for some reason, ran at it<br>
to pet it. As it tried to hop away,<br>
it stumbled and then fell down on its back<br>
all hissing. When she stood above the bird,<br>
it screeched horridly, like a squealing tire,<br>
and burned out, falling limply in the leaves.<br>
A gaggle of a dozen geese arrived<br>
and shooed Marie away. They all looked up<br>
and honked and honked around the lifeless bird.<br>
Marie came back to me and started crying<br>
that she had killed the bird so recklessly.<br>
I sat there thinking how to comfort her<br>
but I just wasn’t ready. Looking back<br>
at all the mourning geese, I saw in them<br>
something so human, so unlike myself:<br>
so genuine, and so deliberate—<br>
something I couldn’t conjure up to give<br>
Marie. Both her and one-legged goose lay down,<br>
but only one of them was being cared for.<br>
While all the waterfowl came flooding in<br>
singing a cacophonous requiem,<br>
I told Marie her tears were all in vain,<br>
that there was nothing else she could have done<br>
about a goose literally on its final leg.<br>
I said that if it wasn’t her that did it,<br>
some child or dog or branch would have sufficed.<br>
The people passing by stared down at us,<br>
but none of them stopped by to ask what’s wrong<br>
and kept on walking, as the honking geese<br>
drowned out the prolonged heaves Marie was giving.<br>
I looked again to see the flock of birds<br>
had grown to twice the number as before.<br>
She calmed down slowly as I sat beside,<br>
dumb in confoundment, hoping I could find<br>
a way of making up for my inaction.<br>
In hoping that, at last, I’d cheer her up,<br>
a pleading silence lingered in the air,<br>
attending some respite. But after all,<br>
I didn’t say a word.<br><br>
She rose, then turned her back, brushed off some leaves,<br>
and started leaving just as wordlessly.<br>
At last, I found my voice and said some muck<br>
that sounded like a shrill pathetic duck<br>
approximating an apology—<br>
but it was all in vain. She turned to me<br>
and asked me something I wasn’t fain<br>
to answer to; something that wracks my brain:<br><br>
“If birds can show more love than you can do,<br>
*how do you know the love you have is true*?”

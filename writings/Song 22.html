Song 22

“On Shakespeare”

Item a capon. 2.s,ii,d.
Item sawce. iiij,d.
Item sacke two gallons. v.s,viij,d.
Item anchaues and sacke after supper. 2,s,vj,d.
Item bread. ob.
- 1 Henry IV, II, v, 487–49

when they turn me into soup
do not pick the vegetables and bones from out my broth
and say “no one would want to have this part”
or “they should not be allowed to have that part”;
by all means, prepare my soup as you want,
but never mislead others into having a fake soup of me.

when they turn me into soup,
keep me on a simmer for as long as you can,
and add bok choy, tomatoes, okra, tamarind,
and whatever else you can think of;
share me around so others can make their own soups of me.

when they turn me into soup,
do not ask “why?” or deny that i am soup.

when they turn me into soup,
consume me however it pleases you to:
with a small wooden spoon,
through a thin metal straw,
with your mouth to the bowl,
or by means of a soggy old sop.

when they turn me into soup,
you need not pretend to love the flavour;
but until you have a sip of it,
only then you may critique or reject
or accept or make a soup of yourself.

when they turn me into soup, remember how i taste!

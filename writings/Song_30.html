Song 30

“I’ll put you in my landscape”

I’ll put you in my landscape:
in amidst a snow-covered forest
where a lone lowing deer tries
freeing its antler from a bush
while you stand in wait nearby
half hidden behind an arching yew
with your bow grasped in your hand
and a greyhound at your feet;
but you pay no heed to it—
something else catches your eye:
your pupils grow
your mouth quavers
you grasp your bow
firmer still;
perhaps some better quarry
not in the forest or the snow;
something— something.
Staring out ashamed that I still see you
—there I will place you.

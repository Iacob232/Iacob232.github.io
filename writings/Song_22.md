### “On Shakespeare”
> <i>Item a capon. 2.s,ii,d.<br>
Item sawce. iiij,d.<br>
Item sacke two gallons. v.s,viij,d.<br>
Item anchaues and sacke after supper. 2,s,vj,d.<br>
Item bread. ob.</i>

--- <i>Henry IV, Part 1</i>, II, v, 487–49<br><br>
When they turn me into soup,<br>
do not pick the vegetables and bones from out the broth<br>
and say “no one would want to have this part”<br>
or “they should not be allowed to have that part”;<br>
by all means, prepare my soup as you want,<br>
but never mislead others into having a fake soup of me.<br><br>
When they turn me into soup,<br>
keep me on a simmer for as long as you can;<br>
add bok choy, tomatoes, okra, tamarind,<br>
and whatever else you can think of;<br>
share me around with others<br>
so they can make their own soups of me.<br><br>
When they turn me into soup,<br>
do not ask “why?” or deny that I am soup.<br><br>
When they turn me into soup,<br>
consume me however it pleases you to:<br>
with a small wooden spoon,<br>
through a thin metal straw,<br>
with your mouth to the bowl,<br>
or by means of a soggy old sop.<br><br>
When they turn me into soup,<br>
you need not pretend to love the flavour;<br>
but until you have a sip of it,<br>
only then you may critique or reject<br>
or accept or make a soup of yourself.<br><br>
When they turn me into soup, remember how I taste!

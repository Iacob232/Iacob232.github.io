A word can change a life. I heard a song
sung by a street performer to a bird
that flew around his head as on he strummed
about the end of times. “Do not delay!
The world is filled with sickness and decay!
And nobody will save you from that day
unless you stop and pray.” The pigeon cooed
unmoved by the invective. People shrugged,
covered their ears, or shuffled through the plaza,
determined to escape the siren song.

The plaza was vacated, save the bird,
myself, and the musician. On he strummed;
his lyrics lost their meaning, and his voice
was buried in the silence of the noise
that emanated from the rattling strings
until it resonated on these words:

“Fɪʀᴇ! Dᴇʟᴜɢᴇ! Oᴄᴇᴀɴꜱ ꜰɪʟʟᴇᴅ ᴡɪᴛʜ ᴠᴇɴᴏᴍ!
Bᴇɢ ꜰᴏʀ ᴍᴇʀᴄʏ! Rᴜɴ! Hɪᴅᴇ! Cʜᴀɴɢᴇ. Hᴇ’ꜱ ᴄᴏᴍɪɴɢ.”

I woke. The pigeon fled. The plaza filled.
The old guitarist vanished into air
as did his voice. He left a plectrum there.
Just as I read the word he scrawled on it
I heard the rouse.
                             It was too late to change.
